CMB1512 - bike: calm at speed, sharp at rest (I had been moving the wrong knob); dragon cut hard

I HAD BEEN TUNING THE WRONG END
Turn rate is  v/R + pivot*(1-speed):
  · the PIVOT term turns you at a standstill and fades out as you gain speed
  · the v/R term turns you AT SPEED and does nothing when parked
Every "make it turn harder" pass tightened R, which only ever helps where it was already sharpest -
so the standstill end stayed where it was and the top end ran away to 287 deg/s, which is the
spin-out. The pivot stays exactly where it is (you said that end is right); R opens back up so
speed now calms the bike instead of amplifying it.

  full lock        full speed    half speed    at rest
    tank            119 deg/s     82 deg/s      67 deg/s
    last build      287           114           ~49        <- unrideable at speed
    now             122            72            38        <- back to tank-like at speed, pivot untouched

THE TWO STICKS ADDING TOGETHER
Strafe and steer both push you sideways, so at speed the left and right sticks were summing into one
muddled lateral shove. Strafe now fades out with speed: full authority for parking and lining up,
mostly gone once you are travelling, where the carve should be doing the work.

SPINNING OUT
Grip had been loosened to 3.1/0.72 for the water feel. That was fine at the old rates and became a
real spin-out once yaw nearly tripled. Pulled back to 4.0/0.55 - it still washes out and carves, it
just catches itself again.

DRAGON
Told twice that it is still enormous, so this is a decisive cut rather than another cautious
halving: 150 -> 68 -> 24, about a sixth of what first shipped. Cruise 88 -> 74 and the ceiling
95 -> 78, so it sits UNDER the manta's ~84 rather than over the level. Small is a cheap mistake to
correct in the other direction; large clearly is not.

Measurement note: the strafe fade is verified arithmetically rather than directly - my probe never
let the bike come to a stop, so both its "parked" and "at speed" samples were taken while coasting
at similar speeds and looked identical. Working back from them (173/(0.95*260)=0.70) gives exactly
the fade factor for the speed it was actually doing, so the term is live.
